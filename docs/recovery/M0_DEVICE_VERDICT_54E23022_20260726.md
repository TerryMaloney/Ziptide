## 3. Confirmed failures and exact ownership

### DV-01 — Home Hub overcorrected too close

**Device:** usable, but the tile row is uncomfortably close.

**Source/log proof:** `HomeHubRuntime` puts the board at 1.5 m but offsets the tiles 1.05 m toward the head, deliberately producing `tileReach=0.45`. The device log prints that exact value. The fix solved liveness but selected the extreme end of direct-hand reach.

```text
HOME_HUB_ANCHOR ... tileReach=0.45
```

**Repair law:** do not revert to one-shot placement and do not remove the direct-hand fallback. Retune through several simple headset proxy distances while retaining live tracked-head anchoring and real-ray selection.

### DV-02 — no pause, return-to-hub, or world-exit path

**Device:** after entering ToxicCity, Terry could not return to the Home Hub or leave the world, preventing the second sword check and a clean second route.

**Source proof:** `CONTROL_SCHEME.md` reserves Y for a future player menu; the active runtime control log lists movement, sprint, crouch/slide, jump, and diagnostic forehead gesture but no production pause/menu/return verb.

This is a missing required player/QA escape path, not a travel regression.

**Repair law:** production needs an accessible pause/menu eventually; recovery builds also need one deterministic in-headset escape route that cannot depend on the computer. The diagnostic world-warp gesture may remain developer-only, but it is not a substitute for a player pause screen.

### DV-03 — Breaker Blade pose points forward instead of vertical

**Device:** sword points forward in a stabbing pose rather than rising vertically from the fist.

**Source proof:** `ItemFactory.CreateArenaWeapon` gives `BreakerBlade` a factory default `gripEuler = (70,0,0)` and describes it as a raised blade. The actual Quest result proves that this axis/value does not match the tracked controller basis. A nonzero definition override could also supersede the factory default, so the definition must be checked before editing.

**Owner:** `ItemDefinition.gripLocalEuler` if nonzero; otherwise `ItemFactory` BreakerBlade default.

**Repair law:** change the melee-specific pose only. Do not modify `GunGripTilt`, gun attach transforms, or generic XRI grab behavior.

### DV-04 — gun laser remains visible while holstered

**Device/image:** gun on belt continues projecting the red laser.

**Root cause proven in source:** `GunLaserSight.Update` shows the line whenever `XRGrabInteractable.isSelected` is true. An `XRSocketInteractor` selects a holstered gun, so a socketed gun satisfies the same condition as a hand-held gun.

```csharp
bool show = _grab != null && _grab.isSelected && _muzzle != null;
```

**Repair law:** the laser must require a selecting **hand/controller interactor**, not merely any selection. Socket selection, restoration parenting, belt transport, and loose state must all hide the line. Preserve held-gun laser behavior.

### DV-05 — R3 produces a jerky camera/body event

**Device:** normal right-stick smooth turn is good; pressing the right stick causes a random-feeling jerky turn/displacement.

**Log proof:** the press is not unowned. R3 is mapped to crouch. At 14:24:55, the same press occurred while sprint was active and triggered all of:

```text
LOCO_STATE sprint=True
LOCO_STATE slide=true
LOCO_STATE crouch=True
LOCO_STATE sprint=False
```

**Source proof:** `DashLocomotion` maps right-stick click to crouch, starts slide when sprinting, lowers the camera parent by 0.55 m, changes CharacterController height, and changes movement speed. Because the same physical stick also controls smooth turning, pressing while deflected combines yaw input with a sudden height/velocity state change. That is the jerky effect Terry perceived.

**Repair law:** treat this as a control/comfort conflict, not a mysterious turn-provider bug. The next control decision must avoid a sudden camera drop/slide on the same stick the player is actively using for smooth yaw, or otherwise require an unambiguous, comfortable activation and suppress accidental combined input.

### DV-06 — hoverbike forward view obstructed and mount UI is placeholder-grade

**Device/images:** large vehicle primitives sit directly in the rider's forward view; giant `RIDE` and `STEP OFF` cubes/labels dominate the vehicle.

**Source proof:** `VehicleRuntime` procedurally creates:

- hoverbike `Fork_L` / `Fork_R` tall primitives near the nose;
- a common `ControlBar` and `DashGlow` in front of the seat;
- two 0.5 × 0.18 × 0.3 m selectable cubes at ±0.9 m labeled `RIDE` and `STEP OFF`;
- an additional large ride label.

These are functioning graybox parts, not production cockpit/mount interaction.

**Repair law:** vehicle presentation and mount interaction need a dedicated measured vehicle pass. Do not simply hide random blue parts without hierarchy confirmation. Establish the eye/camera clearance volume, then remove, reshape, or relocate any primitive intersecting it. Replace giant button cubes with a contextual seat/grab/mount affordance and a comfortable dismount action.

### DV-07 — vehicle yaw ownership is incomplete

**Device:** the hoverbike moves and can mount/dismount, but steering is hard to judge and feels like small player spins rather than the bike cleanly turning.

**Source proof:** while riding, `VehicleRuntime` disables player locomotion/turn providers and feeds the right stick into `FlightInputCore.Shape`; only `frame.YawSnap` is applied. More importantly, `FollowSeat()` copies only the vehicle seat **position** to the persistent rig:

```csharp
_rig.transform.position = transform.TransformPoint(seat);
```

It does not carry the rig through vehicle yaw. The vehicle transform can rotate while the rider's rig orientation remains in world space, producing a disconnected steering/read experience.

**Repair law:** define one vehicle-space mount frame that carries player position and vehicle yaw while preserving local physical-head freedom. Decide smooth versus snap vehicle steering deliberately; do not let vehicle yaw and player-turn providers both own orientation.

### DV-08 — toxic damage boundary does not match the visible liquid

**Device/images:** damage begins before the player visually touches the green toxic liquid.

**Log proof:** two large rectangular volumes are active:

```text
toxic_city_river_1 size=(10.0, 2.7, 12.0)
toxic_city_river_0 size=(14.0, 2.9, 30.0)
```

There were 10 enters, 7 exits, 33 damage pulses, and 3 lethal recoveries.

**Source proof:** `ToxicRiverRuntime` does not test contact against the rendered surface. It samples `rig.position + up*0.25` against an axis-aligned `Bounds` box. Therefore hazard height/edges can extend beyond the visible liquid unless the builder's bounds are perfectly matched.

**Repair law:** bind exposure height and horizontal margin to the authored visible hazard surface, or add an unmistakable secondary danger field that makes the larger boundary truthful. Test standing, crouched, seated, and vehicle samples.
