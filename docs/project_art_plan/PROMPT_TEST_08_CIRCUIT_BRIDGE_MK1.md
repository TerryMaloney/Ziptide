# PROMPT TEST 08 — CIRCUIT BRIDGE MK I

**Status:** controlled visual-prompt experiment only. Equipment name, recipe, progression slot, world use, IDs, and implementation are not canon until separately approved.

**Purpose:** test whether the completed Conduct-family chain can produce a third one-slot field utility with a new verb—bridge / route / discharge—while remaining visually and functionally distinct from the Moss Filter Rig and Cistern Grip Clamp.

## 1. Working equipment role

> The Circuit Bridge Mk I temporarily bridges one broken low-voltage contact, routes current through one dead mechanism, or safely drains one small stored charge while the player remains physically present.

It is not a gun, taser, lightning weapon, remote hacking tool, universal key, battery backpack, grappling device, or combat disable system.

## 2. Working recipe

- one asymmetrical square Equipment Frame Blank;
- one refined Conductive Vein insert;
- no catalyst in Mk I.

A Charge Nodule may appear in a later upgrade or separate controlled-discharge tool. Do not force it into the first equipment recipe.

## 3. Equipment-specific visual grammar

- battered rectangular salvage-metal body;
- one guarded visible biological routing channel;
- graphite-brown segmented Conductive Vein with copper pathways under smoky sheath;
- two short fold-out insulated contact jaws;
- one large VR-friendly bridge lever;
- one mechanical connected/disconnected indicator;
- one belt loop;
- one small keyed service latch;
- restrained violet-white pulse only while current flows;
- palm-to-forearm scale;
- no barrel or forward weapon silhouette.

## 4. Prompt A — production/equipment sheet

> Create a clean production concept sheet for the **Circuit Bridge Mk I**, a compact one-slot belt utility for the grounded family-friendly ZIPTIDE science-fiction game. It is assembled from one asymmetrical square Equipment Frame Blank and one refined Conductive Vein insert. Its function is to temporarily bridge one broken low-voltage contact, route power through one dead mechanism, or safely drain one small stored charge while the player remains beside the device.
>
> Show front, side, belt-mounted, opened-insert replacement, folded-jaw, and active-bridge views. The device has a battered rectangular salvage-metal frame, one protected transparent or heavy-mesh routing channel containing graphite-brown segmented biological vein with visible copper pathways beneath smoky sheath, two short fold-out insulated contact jaws, one large VR-friendly bridge lever, one obvious mechanical connected/disconnected indicator, one belt loop, and one keyed service latch.
>
> In the active view, show both jaws clamped onto two corroded low-voltage terminals while one restrained violet-white pulse travels through the biological channel and an analog gauge wakes. The player’s hands must remain clear of exposed current and moving contacts. The utility should be palm-to-forearm sized and immediately recognizable as equipment rather than a weapon.
>
> Preserve ZIPTIDE's used-future visual language: worn welded metal, repairable fasteners, guarded mechanisms, analog physical state, grounded material realism, restrained emissives and strong child-readable silhouette.
>
> **No gun barrel, trigger, projectile, taser prongs, combat lightning, hacking screen, universal key, grappling line, battery pack, backpack, Moss Filter cartridge, Grip Pad, neon-blue glow, sleek corporate styling, readable text or watermark.**

## 5. Prompt B — player-eye field use

> Create a cinematic but gameplay-readable player-eye concept scene showing the **Circuit Bridge Mk I** used beside a broken low-voltage relay cabinet in a rain-wet ZIPTIDE industrial ruin. The exact world identity remains provisional. Use old masonry, patched metal platforms, puddles, corroded analog junction boxes, amber work lamps, exposed but guarded cables and distant storm light.
>
> Show the player removing the compact Circuit Bridge from one belt slot and placing its two short insulated jaws across a clearly broken relay gap. The player pulls one large bridge lever. A restrained violet-white pulse travels visibly through the guarded biological routing channel and into the cabinet. One analog gauge moves from zero to a safe low reading, one small mechanism activates, and no large electrical arc occurs.
>
> Keep the tool physically local: the player must remain next to the relay, the jaws remain attached, and the tool does not create a remote line or hacking beam. Show one hand operating the lever while the other remains free to turn a nearby mechanical control or retrieve a small component.
>
> Preserve grounded used-future materials, wet industrial atmosphere, readable route composition and family-friendly tension.
>
> **No combat pose, lightning attack, giant arc, taser use, remote hacking, floating UI, quest arrows, bright cyan glow, magical power transfer, readable text or watermark.**

## 6. Prompt C — production-chain consistency sheet

> Create a clean production concept sheet showing the manual manufacture of a **Circuit Bridge Mk I** through the established ZIPTIDE production chain.
>
> Show: 1) one asymmetrical square Equipment Frame Blank produced by the Ore Processor; 2) one refined Conductive Vein insert produced by the BioRefiner, using graphite-brown segmented biological tissue with copper pathways beneath smoky sheath; 3) both components seated in the Assembler Mk I, square industrial socket on the left and round or keyed biological-component holder on the right; 4) protected cradle guard closed before articulated arms move; 5) completed Circuit Bridge returned to the same cradle; 6) finished device attached to one belt slot; 7) active field use bridging two low-voltage terminals and waking one analog gauge.
>
> Preserve the separate machine keepers rather than redesigning them. Make each step understandable through component silhouette and physical position even after all words are removed.
>
> **No Charge Nodule catalyst, third recipe input, generic battery, gun silhouette, taser, magic fabrication beam, machine-body drift, invented corporation, readable text or watermark.**

## 7. Prompt D — child-readable physical equipment card

> Create a physical in-world equipment card for the **Circuit Bridge Mk I** in a family-friendly VR science-fiction game. The card is a sturdy worn salvage-metal or laminated object, not a flat modern menu.
>
> Use a large recognizable silhouette of the Circuit Bridge, one square industrial-frame icon, one Conduct-family icon showing a pulse traveling between two separated contacts, and one simple before/after illustration: a dead analog gauge beside a broken connection, then the same gauge active while the bridge tool joins the gap.
>
> Reserve space for a short visible name and one spoken-function caption, but do not depend on generated words. The card must remain understandable through silhouette, separated/joined contact shapes, gauge position and physical-state imagery. Use no color-only meaning.
>
> **No scrolling UI, dense stats, lightning-bolt weapon icon, hacking symbol, corporate branding, readable generated text or watermark.**

## 8. Evaluation gates

The experiment passes only if:

1. the device reads as a compact utility, not a gun or taser;
2. Conductive Vein anatomy remains visible and biological;
3. fold-out contacts are short, guarded and physically local;
4. active state uses one traveling pulse, not an electrical explosion;
5. analog cause/effect communicates the function;
6. one hand can operate the tool while the other performs the task;
7. device occupies one belt slot;
8. square industrial and biological insert categories remain readable;
9. equipment remains distinct from Filter Rig and Grip Clamp at thumbnail size;
10. downstream measured-spec work can identify grip, latch, lever, contacts, belt mount and protected channel without inventing the core design.

## 9. Run order

1. Prompt A — production/equipment sheet;
2. Prompt B — player-eye field use;
3. if promising, Prompt C — production-chain consistency;
4. Prompt D — accessibility card;
5. use delta-only refinement for no more than three named failures.
