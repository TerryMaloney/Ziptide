# PROMPT TEST 07 — RELAY REED / CONDUCT FAMILY

**Status:** controlled visual-prompt experiment only. The species, exact name, world placement, recipes, field item, and progression slot are not canon until separately approved.

**Purpose:** test whether the ZIPTIDE prompt grammar can create a third biological family with a new functional verb—**conduct / route / discharge**—without copying Moss Filter or Cistern Gripvine anatomy.

## 1. Functional hypothesis

Working species name: **Relay Reed**.

Working biological outputs:

- **Conductive Vein:** flexible copper-dark biological strand that routes low-voltage current;
- **Charge Nodule:** detachable ceramic-glass seed organ that stores a small controlled charge.

Working field invention:

- **Circuit Bridge Mk I:** compact one-slot utility that temporarily bridges a broken low-voltage contact, routes power through one dead mechanism, or safely drains a small stored charge.

This is a utility and puzzle tool, not a gun, lightning weapon, combat taser, or universal hacking device.

## 2. Distinct family grammar

### Shared ZIPTIDE constants

- grounded biological logic;
- useful harvest outputs;
- strong child-readable silhouette;
- restrained emissive activity tied to real state;
- used-future industrial context;
- scale and physical interaction shown;
- no color-only meaning.

### Conduct-family-specific grammar

- living body: narrow graphite-brown segmented reed bundles;
- routing anatomy: visible copper-colored vein paths embedded under translucent smoky skin;
- contact anatomy: forked two-prong leaf tips or contact whiskers, not broad leaves;
- storage organ: small ceramic-white or smoky-glass Charge Nodules with a single visible internal spiral;
- active state: restrained violet-white pulse traveling along one vein at a time;
- growth habit: clustered upright reeds that lean toward nearby powered machinery or storm charge;
- seed: palm-sized split-cylinder puck with two offset contact ridges;
- ship cultivar: below-waist ceramic trellis or insulated rack.

### Explicit exclusions

- no Filter honeycomb membranes or round filter organs;
- no Grip braided tendons, pale suction pads, or amber compression nodes;
- no ordinary bamboo, cattails, flowers, lightning-bolt leaves, Tesla coils, crystal plants, fantasy magic, neon-blue glow, animal faces, or exposed weapon electrodes.

## 3. Prompt A — lifecycle production sheet

> Create a clean production concept sheet for one alien plant species called the **Relay Reed**, a Conduct-family organism for a grounded family-friendly science-fiction game. Show five clearly related forms sharing the exact same anatomy: a palm-sized asymmetric split-cylinder seed puck with two offset contact ridges beside a gloved hand; a small sprout with two forked contact shoots; a knee-high juvenile cluster; a mature wild colony approximately chest-high; and a compact below-waist ship cultivar growing on an insulated ceramic rack. The species uses narrow graphite-brown segmented reed bundles with visible copper-colored biological vein paths beneath smoky translucent skin. Branch tips end in simple forked two-prong contact whiskers rather than ordinary leaves. Mature plants grow several detachable ceramic-white or smoky-glass **Charge Nodules**, each with one visible internal spiral. Show one restrained violet-white pulse traveling along a single vein only where the plant is actively routing charge. Include a small harvested bundle of flexible **Conductive Vein** beside the mature form. Every stage must clearly look like the same organism developing. Used-future 1970s science-fiction biology, grounded material realism, strong child-readable silhouettes, neutral production-sheet background, clear human scale. **No Moss Filter honeycomb anatomy, Cistern Gripvine braided tendons or suction pads, ordinary bamboo, cattails, flowers, crystal plants, Tesla coils, lightning-bolt shapes, neon-blue glow, fantasy magic, animal anatomy, readable text, or watermark.**

## 4. Prompt B — discovery/function scene

> Create a cinematic but gameplay-readable concept scene of a player discovering a **Relay Reed** colony beside a damaged low-voltage relay cabinet in a wet, storm-exposed industrial ruin. The exact world identity is provisional; focus on grounded ZIPTIDE material language: old masonry, patched metal platforms, rainwater, cables, analog junction boxes, amber practical lamps, and distant storm light. The Relay Reeds grow naturally along an insulated wall seam near the cabinet. Their graphite-brown segmented bodies contain visible copper biological veins, and forked contact tips lightly touch two corroded terminals. Show one restrained violet-white pulse traveling through the plant and briefly illuminating a dead analog gauge, visibly demonstrating that the species routes current. Several ceramic-white Charge Nodules hang at reachable height, and one harvested Conductive Vein strand is visible without glowing like a quest marker. The player notices the organism through physical cause and effect, not arrows or UI. Grounded used-future science-fiction, playable route composition, family-friendly but cinematic. **No lightning attack, combat pose, giant electrical arcs, bright cyan quest glow, ordinary reeds, Filter honeycomb tissue, Grip pads, fantasy crystals, readable text, or watermark.**

## 5. Prompt C — harvested components

Show: 1) a hand-sized coil of **Conductive Vein**, made from flexible graphite-brown segmented biological strands with visible copper pathways beneath smoky translucent skin and one clean layered biological cut end; 2) a palm-sized detachable **Charge Nodule**, ceramic-white or smoky glass with one internal spiral and two asymmetrical contact ridges; 3) both components beside a gloved VR hand; 4) the Conductive Vein safely bridging two low-voltage bench contacts; 5) the Charge Nodule seated in a guarded discharge cradle with a mechanical state indicator. Use a restrained violet-white pulse only while current is actively moving. The components must look biological, harvestable, useful, and clearly related to the same species. Grounded production concept art, strong silhouettes, neutral background. **No ordinary copper wire, battery branding, glowing fantasy crystal, Tesla coil, weapon cartridge, Filter anatomy, Grip anatomy, neon-blue glow, readable text, or watermark.**

## 7. Prompt E — Circuit Bridge Mk I equipment test

It must remain palm-to-forearm sized and recognizable as a utility rather than a weapon. **No gun barrel, trigger, projectile, taser prongs, combat lightning, hacking screen, grappling line, battery pack, backpack, Moss Filter cartridge, Grip Pad, neon-blue glow, corporate branding, readable text, or watermark.**

## 8. Evaluation gates

The experiment passes only if:

1. lifecycle stages share one anatomy;
2. trait verb reads as route/conduct before labels;
3. plant remains distinct from Filter and Grip at thumbnail size;
4. emissive activity indicates current movement rather than rarity;
5. outputs look biological rather than store-bought wire/battery;
6. discovery scene shows useful cause and effect;
7. nursery state remains compact and ship-compatible;
8. future field item reads as utility, not weapon;
9. all sources can support stable icons, names, spoken functions, and non-color-only states;
10. a downstream operator could extract measured specs without inventing the core anatomy.

## 9. Run order

1. Prompt A — lifecycle sheet;
2. Prompt B — discovery/function scene;
3. if promising, Prompt C — component sheet;
4. Prompt D — departure/return pair;
5. only after the family closes, Prompt E — field equipment.

Do not repeatedly regenerate a promising composition. Use delta-only refinements naming no more than three concrete failures.
